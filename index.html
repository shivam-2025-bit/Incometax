Understanding Indian Income Tax for FY 2025-26

The Indian income tax system operates under two parallel regimes: the Old Tax Regime and the New Tax Regime. The new regime, introduced in Budget 2020 and revised in 2023 and 2025, offers lower tax rates but doesn't allow most deductions and exemptions that the old regime permits.

New Tax Regime Slabs (FY 2025-26)

Under the new regime, income up to ₹4,00,000 is fully exempt. From ₹4L to ₹8L, a 5% rate applies. ₹8L to ₹12L attracts 10%, and above ₹12L is taxed at 15%, 20%, and 30% progressively. Importantly, income up to ₹12,00,000 attracts zero tax due to the Section 87A rebate.

Old Tax Regime Slabs

The old regime offers a basic exemption of ₹2.5 lakh (₹3L for senior citizens above 60, ₹5L for super seniors above 80). It allows deductions under 80C, 80D, HRA, LTA, and more — making it beneficial for those with high eligible deductions.

Section 87A Rebate

Under the new regime, taxpayers with taxable income up to ₹12,00,000 get a full rebate under Section 87A, effectively paying zero tax. Under the old regime, this rebate is ₹12,500 for those with income up to ₹5,00,000.

Surcharge & Health and Education Cess

A surcharge of 10% applies if income exceeds ₹50 lakh. This rises to 15% above ₹1 crore, 25% above ₹2 crore, and 37% above ₹5 crore. Additionally, a 4% Health and Education Cess is levied on the tax after surcharge.
